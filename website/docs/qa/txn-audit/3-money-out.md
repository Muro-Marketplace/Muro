# Transaction audit 3: money out

Forensic pass over every path by which money leaves the platform or is owed to artists and venues. Scope: the stripe_transfers ledger and its lifecycle (`src/lib/stripe-connect.ts`), the payout capability gate (`src/lib/payouts/capability.ts`), the leg builder (`src/lib/payouts/legs.ts`), the retry cron (`src/app/api/stripe-connect/process-pending`), the refund flows (`src/app/api/refunds/**`, `src/app/api/admin/curation/refund`), paid-loan artist payouts (`src/lib/placements/paid-loan-billing.ts`), and every Stripe event the webhook (`src/app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts`) does or does not handle. All Stripe money-moving calls in the codebase were enumerated: `stripe.transfers.create` (stripe-connect.ts:148), `stripe.transfers.createReversal` (refunds/process/route.ts:255), `stripe.refunds.create` (refunds/process/route.ts:295, admin/curation/refund/route.ts:146,154). Nothing else moves money out.

Date: 2026-08-28. Branch: claude/wallplace-remediation-finish-42e4c1. No source code was modified.

## Invariants checked

**No over-refund.** HOLDS. One open refund request per order (any non-rejected row 409s a new one), the amount is checked against `order.total` at request time and re-asserted at process time after the atomic claim, every reversal is capped at its leg's `amount_cents`, all Stripe calls carry request-scoped idempotency keys, and Stripe itself refuses cumulative refunds beyond the charge, which also makes the curation route's lost-row-update edge safe.

**No double-pay.** BROKEN, two ways. (1) The `transfer.reversed` handler recycles every reversal into a retryable `failed` row that the daily sweep re-executes with an expired idempotency key, re-paying the full leg on any partially refunded order and undoing any dashboard reversal (finding 1). (2) A leg sitting in `failed` at partial-refund time is neither reversed nor cancelled and is later paid in full (finding 4). The paths that were built for this hold: webhook redeliveries collapse onto the UNIQUE (order_id, recipient_user_id) index, `scheduleOrderLegs` re-runs are gap-fillers only, concurrent sweeps collapse onto one Stripe idempotency key, and paid-loan invoices dedupe on the synthetic order id.

**No pay-then-refund-without-reversal.** HOLDS for the in-app path, and strongly: a failed reversal aborts the buyer refund with a 502 (F32), so the platform is never left holding the gap. BROKEN for money moved outside the app: a dashboard refund (`charge.refunded`, finding 5) and a chargeback (`charge.dispute.created`, finding 2) both leave paid legs unreversed and pending legs still scheduled, with the artist paid for a sale the platform did not keep. The inverse also fails: a partial refund before payout takes back the artist's entire leg rather than a proportional share (finding 3).

**Money owed to unready artists held safely.** Half holds. The capture side is genuinely good: all three take-money entry points (cart checkout, offer checkout, paid-loan setup) refuse up front via `canReceivePayout`, which gates on payouts_enabled, fails closed when Stripe is unreachable, and never trusts the stale onboarding boolean; anything that slips through lands as an auditable `blocked` ledger row and the money never leaves the platform. But the blocked state is terminal in practice: nothing requeues it when onboarding completes, no admin surface lists it, refunds do not extinguish it (finding 6), and the paid-loan path bypasses the whole mechanism, retaining the artist's share with no record at all (finding 10).

**Penny arithmetic.** The forward split is exact by construction: integer pence throughout `buildArtistLegs`, deterministic remainder assignment in shipping allocation, residuals absorbed by the platform fee and `assertLegsReconcile` throwing before anything is written. The reverse direction is not symmetric: partial reversals pro-rate the whole leg including its shipping component (finding 8), and per-leg `Math.round` drift on reversals lands on the platform, which is the correct side to bear it.
